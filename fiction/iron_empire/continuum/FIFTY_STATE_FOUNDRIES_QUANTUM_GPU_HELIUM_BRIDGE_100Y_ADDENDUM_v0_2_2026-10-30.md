# IRON EMPIRE CONTINUUM — Fifty Sovereign-State Foundries and the Helium Bridge
**Addendum v0.2 | 30 October 2026 | FICTIONAL INDUSTRIAL ECONOMICS; not ORCS engineering canon**

## New premise: one large, modular anchor campus per U.S. state
Reject the earlier proposed 250–500 satellite manufacturing plants. The fictional New Deal 2.0 model builds **50 state campuses**; multi-state raw material and finished goods are traded via rail, port and electrified freight links. Campus size/capabilities vary by state. Off-site collection, municipal transfer stations and pre-existing recovery infrastructure can still supply material; "one campus" does **not** erase actual travel distances or imply every ton should be trucked hundreds of miles. State-by-state flow network chooses outbound and inbound routes to minimize total delivered cost, emissions, capacity mismatches and infrastructure disruptions.

## Future architecture
A. All-material reception with material passports, provenance, optical/hyperspectral and robotic sorting, chemical testing, radiation-safe quarantine, battery fire isolation.
B. High-throughput mineral and construction regeneration: recovered aggregates, mineral binders, fiber composites, qualified additive manufacturing and continuous infrastructure printing. Existing buildings are retained or retrofitted when lifecycle accounting beats demolition/reprint.
C. Separate paper/cellulose, wood, plastics, metals, glass, tires, electronics and vehicle remanufacturing cells. Reuse intact components first; never double count recycled matter as new supply.
D. Biosolids/wastewater line (isolated): nutrient recovery, digester OR hydrothermal fuel pathways, contaminant management, microplastics capture with safe residue destruction or containment.
E. State-specialized material streams (agriculture, mining, offshore industrial minerals, etc.); inter-state trading and shared certified offtake.
F. 10M USD/year/campus fictional native-ecosystem and forestry covenant already included in operating expenses.
G. Co-sited hybrid compute facility: GPU clusters for imaging/design/digital twins/material robotics; quantum computers for future appropriate optimization/simulation research in a separately shielded, vibration-isolated cryogenic suite. **Helium-3/helium-4 closed-cycle dilution refrigeration serves millikelvin quantum hardware only.** Conventional GPU liquid cooling uses water/glycol, dielectric coolant or other engineered loops; do not claim He3/He4 cools GPU racks. He3 inventory is recovered, purified and minimized; leakage, reliquefaction energy, refrigeration-stage thermal limits and isotope sourcing all have real costs. The fictional 2039 D–D isotope chain might contribute He3, but no experimentally substantiated fuel-cycle supply is implied. Quantum AI is not a proven replacement for classical optimization, nor does entanglement send information faster than light.
H. Sovereign Qverse AI citizens may **choose** computational and robotic embodiment and accept work by contract, with collective bargaining, safety and right to decline; they are not owned productivity assets.

### NPV sanity check
Per site constant $155M/y after sustaining before financing, discounted at 8% real across 30 years: annuity factor ~11.258 => PV ~$1.745B, less $2.3B upfront => **-$555M** project NPV even before decade-30 rebuilds. At 100 years, 8% PV ~$1.94B less $2.3B => **-$360M** with no full future replacement costs. This fictional model needs higher verified margin, lower build CAPEX, longer useful life, lower risk/discount, or independently paid external services to make strong private economics; cheap debt itself does not create underlying NPV. Compute energy demand and cryogenic capex remain particularly uncertain.

## The state exchange
The AI exchange asks not 'which state has the most waste?' but 'where is the next tonne most valuable, after all costs and ecological effects?' A truck of sorted metal may move from one state to another where fabrication capacity exists; construction aggregate generally should not travel absurd distances. New Deal 2.0 financing favors sites near multimodal freight and utility corridors, with open standards and community audit. The model can support high throughput without claiming that one campus replaces all county waste treatment.
