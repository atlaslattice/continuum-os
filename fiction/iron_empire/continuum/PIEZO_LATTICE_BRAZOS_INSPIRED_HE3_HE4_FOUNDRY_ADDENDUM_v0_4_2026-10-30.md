## The architecture
A state-scale circular foundry makes its own compute hardware enclosures and thermal infrastructure from **qualified outputs of recovered demolition materials**: reprocessed metals, remelted suitable glass, ceramics synthesized from verified mineral fractions, and tested recycled polymer/fiber composites. Mixed crushed concrete is never assumed pure enough for cryogenic seals, microfluidics, or electronic-grade components without separate purification, metrology, qualification, and additional processing. Robotic additive and subtractive manufacturing fabricates high-area manifolds, heat exchangers and modular components under individual material certifications.

The system is **inspired by Google Brazos** (modular rack-manifold serviceability and monitoring), but not presented as a physically compatible Brazos replacement:
* **He-4 GPU rack circuit**: fictional fully sealed high-pressure helium gas/cold-plate thermal modules. Piezoelectric sensors, distributed pressure/flow acoustic monitoring, and imaginative piezo-assisted microvalves/actuators are integrated into the manifolds. "Piezo-soaked" is an in-universe shorthand for deeply embedded piezoelectric instrumentation, NOT a claim that piezoelectricity creates free refrigerative cooling or generates net energy. Coolant compressor and heat-sink demands remain explicit. Water and phase-change liquids remain possible engineering benchmarks; helium must win an actual energy/maintenance/thermal comparison to claim real efficiency.
* **He-3/He-4 cryogenic quantum-node circuit**: separate dilution-refrigeration stages with independent helium inventory, isotope separation, cryogenic shielding and extremely small millikelvin heat loads relative to GPU megawatts. He-3 is a working fluid inventory and circulates; it is not consumed at the rate of a reactor isotope production stream. Both He-3 and He-4 matter to the dilution mixture.
* **Thermal cascade**: GPU heat passes to useful low-temperature industrial duties when temperature-appropriate, otherwise proper heat rejection. Quantum cryogenics reject compressor/refrigerator heat to higher-temperature stages; heat doesn't spontaneously move from cold to hot.
* **Autonomy and governance**: Qverse residents independently choose robotic contracts; human-approved safety controls, material provenance, ecological audits and reproducible benchmarks. Quantum algorithms don't automatically outperform classical GPU scheduling.

## Reactor yields — IDEAL nuclear arithmetic, not validated plant performance
**D–D reactor A: 1.1 GW gross fusion**, 50/50 primary branching:
D+D -> T + p (~4.03 MeV)
D+D -> He-3 + n (~3.27 MeV).
Average ~3.65 MeV/primary reaction, 1.1GW / (3.65MeV × 1.602176634e-13 J/MeV) ≈ 1.881e21 DD reactions/s; half produce He-3, half T.
He-3 ~0.5 × 1.881e21 × 3.016u × 1.66054e-27 kg/u × 31,536,000s/y = **~148.6 kg He-3/y**.
T same mass order **~148.6 kg T/y** before burn, collection losses, decay or neutron effects.
**D–T reactor B: 1.1 GW gross fusion**:
D+T -> He-4 + n (17.6 MeV), ~3.90e20 reactions/s → **~81.7 kg He-4/y**.
These are **separate branches**, not 2.2GW each. The older 20MW ARC magnetic-design figure cannot be inserted into the D-D laser reactor. If D-D source power, duty factor, branch ratios, or secondary reactions vary, both isotope yields change; recoverable delivery = birth × operational duty factor × separation efficiency minus downstream use/loss.
**Dual-fusion sum:** ideal primary He-3 + He-4 ~230kg/y combined at continuous target gross powers; may be modest compared with initial facility-wide helium inventory. D-D He-3 has important competition with other planned isotope uses; allocation must be budgeted. T generated from D-D is proposed feed for D-T and must not simultaneously be sold as surplus. None of these calculated outputs verifies sustained laser ignition, tritium self-sufficiency or net plant electricity.

## Inventory and feasibility
He-4 rack gas-loop fill depends on manifold volume, loop pressure and temperature; the 100MW/200MW campus requires ~100/200MW thermal heat rejection plus compressors and the rest of PUE. At 20K bulk temperature rise and helium cp ~5.19kJ/kg/K, ~963/1927kg/s He-4 CIRCULATION, *not consumption*, for 100/200MW. Huge circulation makes this speculative and may be inferior to water cooling.
If ONE dual reactor supports ONE campus and its chosen initial He-4 inventory is 10,000kg, ideal He-4 annual 81.7kg is enough to replenish up to 0.817% inventory losses annually; at 80% recovery ~0.654%. Initial 10,000kg inventory takes ~122 ideal reactor-years if obtained only from that reactor; inventory must be externally seeded or radically smaller. He-3 quantum inventory has separate (much smaller) potential and experimentally determined leak budgets; He-3 production ~148.6kg/y **does not** mean one should consume it in single-pass cooling.
Across 50 campuses, per-campus yield cannot be multiplied without explicitly assuming 50 reactor pairs, each meeting enormous hypothetical gross fusion output, and accounting net electrical conversion/lasers/utilities.
