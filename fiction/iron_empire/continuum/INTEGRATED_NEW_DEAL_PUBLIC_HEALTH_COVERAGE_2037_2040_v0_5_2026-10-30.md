**2037 fictional National Public Coverage Service (NPCS)** offers universal comprehensive single-public-payer insurance to all people legally in scope under the imagined law; does **direct adjudicated payment to licensed private clinicians/hospitals and lawful pharmacies**, preserving professional delivery autonomy, access and evidence-based coverage rules. Private insurance employment and existing insurance obligations require lawfully handled compensation and resolution; do not assume overnight disappearance with no service disruptions. **By 2040 in fiction, ordinary duplicative private health insurance is commercially obsolete** due to public coverage and better service, while narrow supplements might remain legally available depending on fictional law.

## Healthcare revenue and savings accounting: four separate ledgers
1. Funding receipts: household/employer premiums displaced can be redirected to public contributions or payroll taxes by law, but they are **NOT new national income**; private premiums migrating to state collection don't count as new wealth and raise government's GROSS budget massively, with corresponding services funded.
2. Provider/clinical expense: actual care remains expensive and comprehensive benefits may increase utilization. Direct pay may reduce some claim-processing layers but not medical service costs automatically; clinicians must be adequately paid on time and fraud checked.
3. Incremental savings: administrative simplification, pharmaceutical purchasing, avoided uncompensated care, reduced disease-related complications and better preventive coordination — only net-of-implementation measured counterfactual savings enter the fiscal ledger.
4. Household upside: reduction in premiums, medical debt, bankruptcies, time lost and productivity damage counts to social welfare and GDP only once, not again as additional federal revenues.
Illustrative 2024-dollar **static fraction-of-2024-NHE savings screen** (not projected 2040 savings): 3% of $5.3T = $159B/year; 5% = $265B; 10% = $530B, *conditional* on real administrative/price/delivery savings, not assumed evidence and possibly offset by expanded services. Savings would reduce total national health spending relative to the baseline if genuine, not necessarily add an equal amount to federal coffers. Public insurance operating balance = actual dedicated receipts - claims and admin - reserves - transition/outstanding liabilities. Aggregate national value = true reduction in resources consumed for equivalent or better health results, not funding transfer.
No healthcare net saving baked into founding capital or system budget unless specifically scenario gated.
